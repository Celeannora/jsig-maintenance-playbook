| RACI / role responsibilities | - SRE teams are responsible for the availability, latency, performance, efficiency, change management, monitoring, emergency response, and capacity planning of their services.<br>- SRE teams interact with the production environment, product development teams, testing teams, users, and so on.<br>- Google places a 50% cap on the aggregate “ops” work for all SREs: tickets, on-call, manual tasks, etc.<br>- SREs spend the remaining 50% of time on development work.<br>- Operational work can be redirected to product development teams by reassigning bugs and tickets to development managers, [re]integrating developers into on-call pager rotations, and so on.<br>- The SRE team must be in charge of capacity planning, which means they also must be in charge of provisioning.<br>- SREs and product developers monitor and modify a service to improve performance, adding capacity and improving efficiency. |

| Escalation / exception handling | - Alerts signify that a human needs to take action immediately in response to something that is either happening or about to happen, in order to improve the situation.<br>- Tickets signify that a human needs to take action, but not immediately; if a human takes action in a few days, no damage will result.<br>- SREs may redirect excess operational work to the product development teams.<br>- Reassigning bugs and tickets to development managers, [re]integrating developers into on-call pager rotations, and so on.<br>- If more than two events occur regularly per on-call shift, problems can’t be investigated thoroughly and engineers are sufficiently overwhelmed to prevent them from learning from these events.<br>- If on-call SREs consistently receive fewer than one event per shift, keeping them on point is a waste of their time.<br>- Once an error budget is depleted, one example response is to stop releases for the remainder of the quarter; this might not be embraced by a product development team unless mandated by management. |
| Cadence / frequencies / thresholds | - Google caps operational work for SREs at 50% of their time.<br>- Google’s rule of thumb is that an SRE team must spend the remaining 50% of its time actually doing development.<br>- On average, SREs focused on operations work should receive a maximum of two events per 8–12-hour on-call shift.<br>- Postmortems should be written for all significant incidents.<br>- Roughly 70% of outages are due to changes in a live system.<br>- Capacity planning should include regular load testing of the system.<br>- Load shifting is often done multiple times per hour.<br>- 50–60% of SREs are Google Software Engineers.<br>- 40–50% of SREs are candidates very close to Google Software Engineering qualifications, with 85–99% of the skill set required, plus rare technical skills useful to SRE. |
