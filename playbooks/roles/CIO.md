# Chief Information Officer (CIO) — Operational Playbook

> Network-agnostic role playbook. Applies regardless of network architecture, classification enclave, or technology stack. Source: [JSIG §1.5.3](../../reference/JSIG-source/section-1.5-roles-and-responsibilities.md), cross-referenced to [MAINTENANCE-PLAN.md](../../MAINTENANCE-PLAN.md) and the [JSIG control-families INDEX](../../reference/JSIG/control-families/INDEX.md).

## 1. Role Summary

The CIO is an organizational official responsible for designating a CISO, developing and maintaining information security policy, overseeing security-responsible personnel and their training, assisting senior officials with security responsibilities, and reporting annually on the organization's information security program effectiveness. The CIO must be a U.S. citizen and government employee, and works closely with the AO — with support from the Risk Executive (Function) and CISO — to ensure the organization-wide security program is effectively implemented ([JSIG §1.5.3](../../reference/JSIG-source/section-1.5-roles-and-responsibilities.md)).

## 2. JSIG-Defined Responsibilities (verbatim, JSIG §1.5.3)

The CIO is responsible for:
- Designating a Chief Information Security Officer (CISO);
- Developing and maintaining information security policies, procedures, and control techniques to address all applicable requirements;
- Overseeing personnel with significant responsibilities for information security and ensuring that personnel are adequately trained;
- Assisting senior organizational officials concerning their security responsibilities; and
- In coordination with other senior officials, reporting annually to the head of the agency on the overall effectiveness of the organization's information security program, including progress of remedial actions.

The CIO, with the support of the REF and the CISO, works closely with the AO and designated representatives to help ensure that:
- a. An organization-wide information security program is effectively implemented resulting in adequate security for all organizational IS and environments of operation;
- b. Information security considerations are integrated into programming/planning/budgeting cycles, enterprise architectures, and acquisition/system development life cycles;
- c. Information systems are covered by approved security plans and are authorized to operate;
- d. Information security-related activities required across the organization are accomplished in an efficient, cost-effective, and timely manner; and
- e. There is centralized reporting of appropriate information security-related activities.

## 4. Coordination Interfaces

- **CISO:** designates and works through the CISO to carry out FISMA security responsibilities; CISO is CIO's primary liaison to AOs, ISOs, CCPs, and ISSM/ISSO ([JSIG §1.5.3](../../reference/JSIG-source/section-1.5-roles-and-responsibilities.md), [§1.5.4](../../reference/JSIG-source/section-1.5-roles-and-responsibilities.md)).
- **Authorizing Official (AO):** works closely with the AO, supported by the REF and CISO, to ensure the organization-wide security program is implemented and systems are authorized ([JSIG §1.5.3](../../reference/JSIG-source/section-1.5-roles-and-responsibilities.md)).
- **Risk Executive (Function) (REF):** receives REF support in coordinating organization-wide risk visibility ([JSIG §1.5.3](../../reference/JSIG-source/section-1.5-roles-and-responsibilities.md)).
- **Agency/Component Head:** delivers the annual information security program effectiveness report ([JSIG §1.5.3](../../reference/JSIG-source/section-1.5-roles-and-responsibilities.md)).
- **Senior organizational officials:** assists them concerning their security responsibilities ([JSIG §1.5.3](../../reference/JSIG-source/section-1.5-roles-and-responsibilities.md)).

## 5. Key Artifacts Owned/Produced

- Written CISO designation memorandum.
- Organization-wide information security policies, procedures, and control techniques.
- Annual information security program effectiveness report to the agency head.
- Evidence that IS security is integrated into programming/planning/budgeting cycles, enterprise architectures, and SDLC.
- Centralized security-activity reporting roll-ups (informed by PM-4/PM-5/PM-6 outputs).
