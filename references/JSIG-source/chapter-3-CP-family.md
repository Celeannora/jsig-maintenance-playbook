| CP-1 CONTINGENCY PLANNING POLICY AND PROCEDURES | **Control:** The organization develops, documents, and disseminates to [Assignment: organization-defined personnel or roles]:<br><br>a. A contingency planning policy that addresses purpose, scope, roles, responsibilities, management commitment, coordination among organizational entities, and compliance; and<br><br>b. Procedures to facilitate the implementation of the contingency planning policy and associated contingency planning controls;<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** Contingency planning policy and procedures address the controls in the CP family that are implemented within systems and organizations. The contingency planning policy can be included as part of the general information security policy for the organization. Contingency planning procedures can be developed for the security program in general, and for a particular information system, when required. Procedures at the system level can be included in the security plan for a system or in one or more separate contingency planning documents that are referenced from the security plan.<br><br>**Related Controls:** PM-9, PM-11, SA-14.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D. |
| CP-2 CONTINGENCY PLAN | **Control:** The organization:<br><br>a. Develops a contingency plan for the information system that:<br><br>1. Identifies essential missions and business functions and associated contingency requirements;<br><br>2. Provides recovery objectives, restoration priorities, and metrics;<br><br>3. Addresses contingency roles, responsibilities, assigned individuals with contact information;<br><br>4. Addresses maintaining essential missions and business functions despite an information system disruption, compromise, or failure;<br><br>5. Addresses eventual, full information system restoration without deterioration of the security safeguards originally planned and implemented; and<br><br>6. Is reviewed and approved by [Assignment: organization-defined official or role].<br><br>b. Distributes copies of the contingency plan to [Assignment: organization-defined key contingency personnel (identified by name and/or by role)];<br><br>c. Coordinates contingency planning activities with incident handling activities;<br><br>d. Reviews the contingency plan for the information system [Assignment: organization-defined frequency];<br><br>e. Updates the contingency plan to address changes to the organization, information system, or environment of operation and problems encountered during contingency plan implementation, execution, or testing;<br><br>f. Protects the contingency plan from unauthorized disclosure and modification.<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** Contingency plans are manifestations of the organization's contingency strategy. Contingency planning includes activities that are required to be performed before, during, and after a system disruption or failure. Examples of contingency plans include business continuity plans, disaster recovery plans, and continuity of operations plans. The contingency plan coordinates contingency planning activities with incident handling activities; addresses contingency roles, responsibilities, assigned individuals with contact information, and specific activities associated with system recovery, reconstitution, and alternative processing; and identifies the applications and data critical to the organization's essential missions and business functions. The contingency plan for the information system is consistent with the organization's business continuity plan. Organizations coordinate contingency planning activities with incident handling activities to ensure that these activities are performed in a coordinated manner, and not in isolation or duplication of effort. The contingency plan may be augmented by one or more separate contingency planning documents. The contingency plan is reviewed and approved by designated officials within the organization with responsibility for the information system. The contingency plan should be distributed to key contingency personnel. The contingency plan is reviewed and updated as necessary to address changes to the organization, information system, or environment of operation and problems encountered during contingency plan implementation, execution, or testing. Protecting the contingency plan from unauthorized disclosure and modification helps to ensure the security of the organization's contingency capabilities. Methods and techniques to protect the contingency plan include storing the plan at off-site locations and controlling access to the plan.<br><br>**Related Controls:** CP-4, CP-6, CP-7, CP-8, CP-9, CP-10, CP-11, CP-13, IR-4, IR-8, PE-17, PM-8.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D.<br><br>**Assignment and Selection Parameters:**<br>CP-2a(6): [Assignment: organization-defined official or role] = [Selection (one or more): senior official, authorizing official, mission/business owner, information system owner, chief information officer, program manager, information system security officer, information system security manager, contingency planning coordinator, other organization-defined official or role].<br><br>CP-2b: [Assignment: organization-defined key contingency personnel (identified by name and/or by role)] = [Selection (one or more): emergency notification contacts, alternate site personnel, information system owners, information system security officers, information system security managers, program managers, system administrators, information owners/stewards, mission/business owners, other organization-defined personnel or roles].<br><br>CP-2d: [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] = [Selection (one or more): at least annually, after significant changes to the information system or environment of operation, after activation of the contingency plan, other organization-defined frequency]. |

The contingency plan is implemented in a coordinated manner with incident response and business continuity/disaster recovery activities.<br><br>**Related Controls:** AT-3, CP-2, CP-4, IR-2, IR-3.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D.<br><br>**Assignment and Selection Parameters:**<br>CP-3a1: [Assignment: organization-defined time period] = [Selection: within 30 days].<br><br>CP-3a3: [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] = [Selection: at least annually].<br><br>CP-3b: [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] = [Selection: at least annually].<br><br>CP-3b: [Assignment: organization-defined events] = [Selection (one or more): activation of the contingency plan, changes to the contingency plan, changes to the information system, changes to the environment of operation, other organization-defined events]. |
| CP-4 CONTINGENCY PLAN TESTING | **Control:** The organization:<br><br>a. Tests the contingency plan for the information system [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] using [Assignment: organization-defined tests] to determine the effectiveness of the plan and the organizational readiness to execute the plan;<br><br>b. Reviews the contingency plan test results; and<br><br>c. Initiates corrective actions, if needed.<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** Methods for contingency plan testing include tabletop exercises, functional exercises, failover tests, and full-scale exercises. A variety of tests can be used to determine the effectiveness of the contingency plan. Testing options include the use of a full-scale test, parallel tests, and tabletop exercises. The contingency plan is tested at the organizational level and at the system level. The organization uses test results to determine the effectiveness of the plan and the organizational readiness to execute the plan. Corrective actions are initiated, as needed, to ensure that contingency plan deficiencies are addressed.<br><br>**Related Controls:** CP-2, CP-3, CP-5, CP-6, CP-7, CP-8, CP-9, CP-10, CP-11, CP-13, IR-3, IR-4, IR-8.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D.<br><br>**Assignment and Selection Parameters:**<br>CP-4a: [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] = [Selection (one or more): annually, semiannually, quarterly, after major system changes, after contingency plan activation, other organization-defined frequency].<br><br>CP-4a: [Assignment: organization-defined tests] = [Selection (one or more): tabletop exercises, functional exercises, failover tests, parallel tests, full-scale exercises, other organization-defined tests]. |

| CP-6 ALTERNATE STORAGE SITE | **Control:** The organization provides and maintains a current, operational alternate storage site that is compliant with the contingency plan and is separate from the primary site so as not to be susceptible to the same hazards and vulnerabilities as the primary site.<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** Alternate storage sites are employed to store backup information, data, and files needed for the recovery and reconstitution of information systems and constituent parts. Alternate storage sites are geographically separated from the primary storage site to reduce common points of failure. Alternate storage site capacity reflects requirements in the contingency plan.<br><br>**Related Controls:** CP-2, CP-7, CP-8, CP-9, CP-10, PE-17.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D.<br><br>**Assignment and Selection Parameters:**<br>CP-6: The alternate storage site is [Selection (one or more): hot, warm, cold]. |

| CP-8 TELECOMMUNICATIONS SERVICES | **Control:** The organization establishes alternate telecommunications services including necessary agreements to permit the resumption of information system operations for the organization’s essential missions and business functions within [Assignment: organization-defined time period] when the primary telecommunications capabilities are unavailable at either the primary or alternate processing or storage sites.<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** Alternate telecommunications services reflect the priorities of contingency requirements in the contingency plan. The organization establishes alternate telecommunications services that are separate from primary telecommunications services so as not to be susceptible to the same hazards and vulnerabilities as the primary telecommunications services. Alternate telecommunications services include a variety of methods such as additional service agreements with primary service providers, contracts with alternate service providers, and satellite communications.<br><br>**Related Controls:** CP-2, CP-4, CP-6, CP-7, CP-9, CP-10, PE-17, SC-7.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D.<br><br>**Assignment and Selection Parameters:**<br>CP-8: [Assignment: organization-defined time period] = [Selection: within 24 hours]. |
| CP-9 INFORMATION SYSTEM BACKUP | **Control:** The organization:<br><br>a. Conducts backups of user-level and system-level information contained in the information system [Assignment: organization-defined frequency];<br><br>b. Conducts backups of system-level information contained in the information system [Assignment: organization-defined frequency];<br><br>c. Protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of backup information at storage locations.<br><br>d. Provides for the timely recovery of system-level and user-level information in the event of a disruption or failure; and<br><br>e. Tests backup information [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] to verify media reliability and information integrity.<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** System-level information includes information system documentation, operating system software, and configuration settings. Backup information includes information residing on magnetic media, optical disks, and other digital media. Backup information can be protected in a number of ways including access controls, cryptographic mechanisms, and physically securing the media at the backup storage location. The organization determines the method of backup based on the protection needs of the information and the information system. Backup information is periodically tested to ensure that the backup information can be reliably restored. The organization may choose to use a variety of backups including incremental, differential, and full backups.<br><br>**Related Controls:** CP-2, CP-4, CP-6, CP-7, CP-8, CP-10, MP-4, MP-5, MP-6.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D.<br><br>**Assignment and Selection Parameters:**<br>CP-9a: [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] = [Selection: daily, weekly, monthly, or other organization-defined frequency].<br><br>CP-9b: [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] = [Selection: daily, weekly, monthly, or other organization-defined frequency].<br><br>CP-9e: [Assignment: organization-defined frequency] = [Selection: weekly, monthly, or other organization-defined frequency]. |
| CP-10 INFORMATION SYSTEM RECOVERY AND RECONSTITUTION | **Control:** The organization provides for the recovery and reconstitution of the information system to a known state after a disruption, compromise, or failure.<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** Recovery and reconstitution include reinstallation, patching, upgrading, or other actions to return the information system to a state that is known to be secure. The organization defines, documents, and tests the actions to be taken to restore the information system to a known state following a disruption, compromise, or failure. The actions are consistent with the contingency plan.<br><br>**Related Controls:** CP-2, CP-4, CP-6, CP-7, CP-8, CP-9, IR-4, SI-2.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D. |
| CP-11 ALTERNATE COMMUNICATIONS PROTOCOLS | **Control:** The organization provides alternative communications protocols to permit the resumption of information system operations for the organization’s essential missions and business functions within [Assignment: organization-defined time period] when the primary communications protocols are unavailable.<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** The organization may employ multiple communications protocols to support the continuation of essential missions and business functions. | CP-12 SAFE MODE | **Control:** The information system, when [Assignment: organization-defined conditions], enters a safe mode of operation with [Assignment: organization-defined restrictions of safe mode operation].<br><br>**Supplemental Guidance:** Safe mode is a mode of operation in which the information system is not performing its full range of functions. A safe mode of operation can be used to reduce the potential for damage and to facilitate recovery. The safe mode can be implemented by the information system or manually by users or administrators.<br><br>**Related Controls:** CP-2, CP-4, CP-10, SI-13.<br><br>**Priority and Baseline Allocation:**<br>Priority and baseline allocation for selected security controls in the contingency planning family are described in Appendix D.<br><br>**Assignment and Selection Parameters:**<br>CP-12: [Assignment: organization-defined conditions] = [Selection: detected attacks, failures, or other adverse conditions].<br><br>CP-12: [Assignment: organization-defined restrictions of safe mode operation] = [Selection: a subset of the full system functionality sufficient to support mission-essential functions, other organization-defined restrictions]. |
