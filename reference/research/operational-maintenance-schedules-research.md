# Operational Maintenance Schedule Research for JSIG / NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 4 Programs

## Scope and framing

This compilation gathers documented, real-world operational cadences that can inform a JSIG-style system administration maintenance program, with emphasis on NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 4/RMF control families used in SAP environments. JSIG itself states RMF Step 6 as monitoring security controls in the information system “on an ongoing basis,” including assessing control effectiveness, documenting system/environment changes, conducting security impact analyses, and reporting security state to designated officials; it does **not** prescribe a universal daily/weekly/monthly schedule for each maintenance activity ([DCSA JSIG 2016](https://www.dcsa.mil/portals/91/documents/ctp/nao/JSIG_2016April11_Final_(53Rev4).pdf)). NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 4 also uses many organization-defined parameters for frequency and time periods, so the most actionable cadence evidence comes from Federal implementations such as FedRAMP, IRS Publication 1075, CMS/HHS ARS, GSA guides, DoD Cyber Discipline/IAVM material, and public STIG examples ([NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 4](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/specialpublications/nist.sp.800-53r4.pdf)). NIST SP 800-137 emphasizes that “continuous” and “ongoing” monitoring means controls and risks are assessed at a frequency sufficient to support risk-based decisions, and it explicitly notes that monitoring frequencies such as annually, quarterly, monthly, and daily are not static or uniform across metrics ([NIST SP 800-137](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/nistspecialpublication800-137.pdf)).

## Control-family findings

### MA — Maintenance

NIST SP 800-53/JSIG-style MA controls often require documented procedures and organization-defined timing rather than fixed periodic schedules, so the strongest documented cadence anchors are annual maintenance-tool review and event-based scheduling of individual maintenance windows. NIST SP 800-40 says each maintenance plan should define actions for each maintenance group, including the timeframes for beginning and ending each action, which maps operationally to MA-2/MA-6 plus CM-3/SI-2 change and patch workflows ([NIST SP 800-40 Rev. 4](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-40r4.pdf)). FedRAMP Moderate and High both assign MA-3 review of previously approved system maintenance tools “at least annually,” making annual tool review a documented Federal cloud benchmark for maintenance-tool control ([FedRAMP Moderate SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-Moderate-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx), [FedRAMP High SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-High-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx)). NIST SP 800-137 specifically uses non-local maintenance as an example where risk may drive “more frequent monitoring” of records kept on nonlocal maintenance and diagnostic activities, but it does not give a numeric interval for MA-4 ([NIST SP 800-137](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/nistspecialpublication800-137.pdf)).

| Non-local maintenance record monitoring | More frequent when risk assessment identifies MA-4 issues; no numeric baseline | MA-4, CA-7 | [NIST SP 800-137](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/nistspecialpublication800-137.pdf) |

**Gap note:** No public JSIG source found in this research specifies a fixed monthly/quarterly MA-2 controlled-maintenance cadence; maintenance events should therefore be scheduled, approved, documented, and closed per change/maintenance tickets, with periodic reviews anchored to documented annual MA-3 tool review and risk-based MA-4 monitoring.

### CM — Configuration Management

FedRAMP control baselines provide concrete CM cadences that are directly usable as SSP/ATO-style schedule examples: annual baseline-configuration review, monthly inventory review, quarterly authorized-software review, and quarterly privilege reevaluation for change-relevant CM-5(5). FedRAMP Moderate/High assign CM-2 baseline configuration review “at least annually and when a significant change occurs,” CM-8 inventory review “at least monthly,” CM-7(5) software list review “at least quarterly or when there is a change,” CM-7(1) annual review for unnecessary/nonsecure functions, ports, protocols, software, and services, and CM-5(5) quarterly privilege review/reevaluation ([FedRAMP Moderate SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-Moderate-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx), [FedRAMP High SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-High-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx)). NIST SP 800-40 raises the bar for inventory freshness by recommending that organizations “constantly maintain” up-to-date software inventories and stating that monthly or quarterly vulnerability-scan-based inventory updates were once acceptable but “should no longer be used” as the main model ([NIST SP 800-40 Rev. 4](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-40r4.pdf)). IRS Publication 1075 gives another Federal example with annual baseline review, three-year retention of configuration-controlled change records, monthly Configuration Control Board meetings when changes are proposed, annual authorized software/hardware list reviews, annual component inventory review, and semiannual inventory-record review for computers and electronic/removable media ([IRS Publication 1075](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1075.pdf)).

### AU — Audit and Accountability

The strongest cross-source convergence for AU-6 is weekly audit-log review. FedRAMP Low, Moderate, and High all assign AU-6 review and analysis of audit records “at least weekly” ([FedRAMP Low SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-Low-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx), [FedRAMP Moderate SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-Moderate-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx), [FedRAMP High SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-High-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx)). IRS Publication 1075 also requires weekly review and analysis of system audit records, while a public STIG finding requires audit-trail review every seven days or more frequently if the site Security Log Management policy requires it ([IRS Publication 1075](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1075.pdf), [STIG Viewer z/OS TSS finding V-3331](https://www.stigviewer.com/stig/zos_tss/2017-03-22/finding/V-3331)). NIST SP 800-137 provides examples where biweekly audit-record analyses may be increased to weekly when anomalous events increase, reinforcing that weekly is a common risk-escalation endpoint rather than a NIST-mandated universal minimum ([NIST SP 800-137](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/nistspecialpublication800-137.pdf)).

### SI — System and Information Integrity

SI cadence evidence splits into vulnerability scanning, patch/flaw remediation, and malicious-code protection. FedRAMP Moderate/High provide a practical baseline of monthly vulnerability scanning, 30/90/180-day remediation for high/moderate/low vulnerabilities, SI-2 software/firmware updates within 30 days of release, SI-2(2) monthly update-status checks, and SI-3 weekly plus real-time malware scanning ([FedRAMP Moderate SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-Moderate-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx), [FedRAMP High SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-High-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx)). FedRAMP’s Continuous Monitoring Playbook similarly requires monthly OS, web application, and database scans and monthly vulnerability-scan submissions, while RFC-0012 documents a more aggressive current direction with at least monthly vulnerability reports, 24-month report history, internet-reachable assessment continuously or at least every three calendar days, non-internet unauthenticated assessment at least every seven calendar days, non-internet authenticated assessment monthly, and mitigation/remediation within 3, 7, or 21 calendar days depending exploitability and exposure ([FedRAMP Continuous Monitoring Playbook](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/documents/Continuous_Monitoring_Playbook.pdf), [FedRAMP RFC-0012](https://www.fedramp.gov/rfcs/0012/)). DoD’s Cybersecurity Discipline Implementation Plan gives a DoD-specific patch objective of current patches within 21 days of IAVA patch release and removal from the network for systems with high-risk security weaknesses over 120 days overdue ([DoD Cybersecurity Discipline Implementation Plan](https://dodcio.defense.gov/portals/0/documents/cyber/cyberdis-impplan.pdf)).

## Cross-source observations and caveats

The strongest documented “core maintenance calendar” pattern for JSIG-style programs is monthly vulnerability/inventory/POA&M operations, weekly audit-log review, annual independent assessment/contingency/IR/control documentation reviews, and risk/event-driven escalation for patches, incidents, and significant changes ([FedRAMP Continuous Monitoring Playbook](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/documents/Continuous_Monitoring_Playbook.pdf), [FedRAMP Moderate SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-Moderate-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx), [IRS Publication 1075](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1075.pdf)). The most material variance appears in vulnerability remediation and access review: FedRAMP Moderate/High use 30/90/180-day vulnerability remediation and monthly-to-quarterly privileged account review depending impact, while DoD IAVA patch practice uses a 21-day patch objective and IRS uses semiannual privileged account review with 90-day user-authenticator refresh ([FedRAMP High SSP Appendix A](https://www.fedramp.gov/resources/templates/SSP-Appendix-A-High-FedRAMP-Security-Controls.docx), [DoD Cybersecurity Discipline Implementation Plan](https://dodcio.defense.gov/portals/0/documents/cyber/cyberdis-impplan.pdf), [IRS Publication 1075](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1075.pdf)). Public JSIG, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 4, and NIST SP 800-137 generally do not define one universal cadence per control; they establish organization-defined, documented, and risk-based frequencies that an SSP/continuous monitoring plan must justify and execute ([DCSA JSIG 2016](https://www.dcsa.mil/portals/91/documents/ctp/nao/JSIG_2016April11_Final_(53Rev4).pdf), [NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 4](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/specialpublications/nist.sp.800-53r4.pdf), [NIST SP 800-137](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/nistspecialpublication800-137.pdf)).
